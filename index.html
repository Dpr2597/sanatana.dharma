Śruti vs. Smṛti

Śruti ("that which is heard") represents primordial, eternal cosmic revelations cognized by ancient Ṛṣis in deep dhyāna (The Vedas & Upanishads). Smṛti ("that which is remembered") comprises traditions, epics, and civic guidelines adapted for each era (Itihāsas, Purāṇas, and Dharmaśāstras).

⚖️

The Four Puruṣārthas

The balanced aims of human existence: Dharma (righteous duty and cosmic harmony), Artha (ethical material wealth and security), Kāma (aesthetic and sensory fulfillment in accord with dharma), and Mokṣa (spiritual liberation from cyclical existence).

🌀

Karma & Saṁsāra

The infallible cosmic law of cause and effect: every volition generates impressions (saṁskāras). Reincarnation (saṁsāra) is the educational journey of the individual self (Jīva) evolving until it realizes its non-dual identity with supreme Consciousness (Brahman).

The 6 Orthodox Systems of Philosophy

All six Darśanas accept the authority of the Vedas while examining reality through distinct epistemological lenses.

1. Nyāya (न्याय)

Sage Gautama • Epistemology & Logic

Establishes rigorous rules of reasoning and four valid sources of knowledge (Pramāṇas): Perception (Pratyakṣa), Inference (Anumāna), Comparison (Upamāna), and Testimony (Śabda).

2. Vaiśeṣika (वैशेषिक)

Sage Kaṇāda • Atomic Metaphysics

Analyzes the physical universe into atoms (Paramāṇu) and categorizes all existing things into distinct ontological categories (Padārthas).

3. Sāṁkhya (सांख्य)

Sage Kapila • Dualistic Enumeration

Delineates reality between unattached pure Consciousness (Puruṣa) and the evolving material creative matrix (Prakṛti) governed by the 3 Guṇas.

4. Yoga (योग)

Sage Patañjali • Practical Self-Realization

The practical discipline of calming the modifications of the mind (Citta-vṛtti-nirodha) via the 8 Limbs (Aṣṭāṅga Yoga) culminating in Samādhi.

5. Mīmāṁsā (मीमांसा)

Sage Jaimini • Ritual Inquiry & Hermeneutics

Examines the early portions of the Vedas (Karmakāṇḍa), the efficacy of sacred sound, and the duty of preserving cosmic order through conscious action.

6. Vedānta (वेदान्त)

Sage Bādarāyaṇa • The Culmination

Inquiry into the ultimate nature of the Self and Brahman as revealed in the Upanishads. Branching into Advaita (Non-dualism), Viśiṣṭādvaita, and Dvaita.

The Four Primordial Vedas (चतुर्वेदाः)

Regarded as Apauruṣeya (authorless, of divine origin), the Vedas are the foundational roots of Sanātana Dharma.

10,552 Mantras • 10 Mandalas Upaveda: Āyurveda

1. Rigveda (ऋग्वेद)

The Veda of Sacred Hymns of Praise

The oldest surviving text of humanity. Contains profound hymns to deities symbolizing cosmic and internal forces (Agni, Indra, Varuṇa, Sūrya, Uṣas). Includes the famous Gāyatrī Mantra (3.62.10) and the sublime Nāsadīya Sūkta (Creation Hymn, 10.129).

Mahāvākya: प्रज्ञानं ब्रह्म (Prajñānaṁ Brahma — "Consciousness is Brahman" — Aitareya Upaniṣad).
1,875 Verses • Melodic Chants Upaveda: Gāndharvaveda

2. Samaveda (सामवेद)

The Veda of Melodies and Sacred Chants

The foundation of Indian classical music. In the Bhagavad Gītā (10.22), Lord Krishna declares: "Among the Vedas, I am the Sāmaveda." Its verses are set to precise musical notation (Sāmagāna) designed to awaken devotional ecstasies and harmonize neural vibrations.

Mahāvākya: तत्त्वमसि (Tat Tvam Asi — "That Thou Art" — Chāndogya Upaniṣad).
Śukla & Kṛṣṇa Recensions Upaveda: Dhanurveda

3. Yajurveda (यजुर्वेद)

The Veda of Liturgical Action & Yajña

Provides the precise formulas, sacred prose, and philosophical justifications for rituals and civic obligations. Divided into Śukla (White) Yajurveda (clear mantras revealed to Sage Yājñavalkya) and Kṛṣṇa (Black) Yajurveda (mantras interwoven with commentaries). Contains the celebrated Śrī Rudram.

Mahāvākya: अहं ब्रह्मास्मि (Ahaṁ Brahmāsmi — "I am Brahman" — Bṛhadāraṇyaka Upaniṣad).
730 Hymns • 20 Kāṇḍas Upaveda: Sthāpatyaveda

4. Atharvaveda (अथर्ववेद)

The Veda of Daily Living, Healing & Statecraft

Attributed to sages Atharvan and Aṅgiras. Blends practical living, herbal medicine, sociological harmony, environmental reverence (the famous Pṛthvī Sūkta — Hymn to Mother Earth), and deeply esoteric spiritual revelations.

Mahāvākya: अयमात्मा ब्रह्म (Ayam Ātmā Brahma — "This Self is Brahman" — Māṇḍūkya Upaniṣad).

The 4 Sequential Layers of Each Veda

1. Saṁhitā (संहिता)

Poetic hymns, chants, and core sonic mantras sung during rituals.

2. Brāhmaṇa (ब्राह्मण)

Prose commentaries detailing the esoteric symbolism and execution of duties.

3. Āraṇyaka (आरण्यक)

"Forest texts" written for hermits focusing on internal meditative sacrifice.

4. Upaniṣad (उपनिषद्)

The philosophical climax (Vedānta) revealing the oneness of Ātman and Brahman.

The Mukhya Upaniṣads (प्रमुख उपनिषदः)

The 10-12 principal philosophical summits commented upon by Ādi Śaṅkara.

The 18 Mahā Purāṇas (अष्टादश महापुराणानि)

The grand encyclopedic storehouse of cosmic history, genealogies, and dharma.

The Itihāsas: Ramayana & Mahabharata

"Itihāsa" literally means 'Thus verily it occurred' — epic living demonstrations of Dharma in human life.

Vālmīki Rāmāyaṇa

24,000 Verses • 7 Kāṇḍas • Composed by Ādikavi Vālmīki

The epic journey of Sri Rama, the exemplar of righteousness (Maryādā Puruṣottama), demonstrating how adherence to truth and duty transcends personal convenience in every relationship—as a son, brother, husband, king, and friend.

The 7 Kandas (Books)
1. Bāla Kāṇḍa

Birth of Sri Rama, training with Vishvamitra, slaying of Tataka, breaking Shiva's bow, marriage to Sita.

2. Ayodhyā Kāṇḍa

Kaikeyi's boons, Dasharatha's heartbreak, Rama's noble acceptance of 14 years exile, Bharata's padukas.

3. Āraṇya Kāṇḍa

Forest hermitage life, Surpanakha incident, golden deer deception, Sita's abduction, Jatayu's sacrifice.

4. Kiṣkindhā Kāṇḍa

Alliance with Sugriva, meeting Hanuman, the slaying of Vali, mobilization of the Vanara search armies.

5. Sundara Kāṇḍa

Hanuman's heroic leap across the ocean, locating Sita in Ashoka Vatika, burning of Lanka, message of hope.

6. Yuddha Kāṇḍa

Building Rama Setu, epic war with Ravana, Vibhishana's surrender, victory of Dharma, return to Ayodhya.

7. Uttara Kāṇḍa

Rama Rajya, the rule of ideal justice, the birth and valour of Lava and Kusha, Sita's sacred reunion with Earth.

Mahābhārata & Śrīmad Bhagavad Gītā

100,000+ Verses • 18 Parvas • Authored by Maharṣi Vedavyāsa

The grandest epic in human history, examining every subtle facet of human psychology, statecraft, ethics, and destiny. Embedded within the Bhīṣma Parva is the Bhagavad Gītā: Lord Krishna's 700-verse discourse to Arjuna on the battlefield of Kurukṣetra.

The Four Primary Paths of Liberation (Yogas)
Karma Yoga

Selfless action performed as worship without selfish attachment to fruits (Karmanyevādhikāraste mā phaleṣu kadācana).

Bhakti Yoga

Loving surrender of ego and emotions to the Divine (Patraṁ puṣpaṁ phalaṁ toyaṁ yo me bhaktyā prayacchati).

Jñāna Yoga

The path of philosophical discrimination separating the eternal Real (Ātman) from the transient unreal.

Rāja / Dhyāna Yoga

Mastery of the mind, breath, and inner energies through steady, unwavering meditation.

Sacred Stories Vault (कथा मञ्जरी)

Timeless spiritual allegories carrying moral, psychological, and cosmological truths.

Cosmic Chronology & Sage Lineage (इतिहास)

The cyclical nature of time and the immortal torchbearers of Sanātana wisdom.

The Four Cosmic Epochs (Caturyuga)

1. Satya / Kṛta Yuga

Golden Age of Truth (1,728,000 years)

Dharma stands on all four legs. Humans possess natural spiritual vision; primary spiritual discipline is intense Dhyāna (meditation).

2. Tretā Yuga

Silver Age (1,296,000 years)

Dharma stands on three legs. Emergence of Sri Rama; primary spiritual discipline is Yajña (conscious sacrifice).

3. Dvāpara Yuga

Bronze Age (864,000 years)

Dharma stands on two legs. Emergence of Sri Krishna and the Kurukshetra war; primary spiritual discipline is Archana (temple worship).

4. Kali Yuga

Current Age (432,000 years)

Dharma stands on one leg. Material distraction reigns; however, liberation is easily attained simply through Nāmasmaraṇa (chanting divine names) and ethical action.

Pivotal Luminaries & Revitalizers

The Saptarṣis (The Seven Primordial Sages)

Agastya, Vasiṣṭha, Viśvāmitra, Jamadagni, Gautama, Atri, and Bharadvāja—the spiritual patriarchs who received Vedic mantras and carried them across geographies.

Maharṣi Vedavyāsa

Organized the unified Veda into the Four Saṁhitās, composed the Mahābhārata, the Brahma Sūtras, and the 18 Purāṇas to preserve dharma across changing yugas.

Ādi Śaṅkarācārya (8th Century CE)

Revived Advaita Vedānta across the length of Bhārata through razor-sharp debates, commented on the Prasthānatrayī, and established the 4 Amnāya Pīṭhams (Śṛṅgerī, Purī, Dvārakā, Joshīmaṭha).

Śrī Rāmānujācārya & Śrī Madhvācārya (11th–13th Century CE)

Champions of Viśiṣṭādvaita (qualified non-dualism) and Dvaita (dualistic devotion), bringing social inclusion, temple reforms, and devotional theology to millions.

The Bhakti Movement & Modern Renaissance

From Kabīr, Mīrābāī, Tulsīdās, and Caitanya Mahāprabhu to Swami Vivekananda and Sri Aurobindo—democratizing spiritual attainment and translating timeless truths for global consciousness.
